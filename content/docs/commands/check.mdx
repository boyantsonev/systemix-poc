A referee with one rulebook for every team. It designs nothing: it reads `design/rules.md` and
says, rule by rule, what passes, what needs a person's eye and what must be fixed, with where and
how. Everything runs on your machine; nothing is sent.

```
/systemix:check [files or folders] [--surface product|marketing|email] [--drift] [--deep] [--score]
```

With no files named, it checks the files changed on your branch.

## What it does

1. **Reads `design/rules.md`, fresh.** No copy of a rule lives in the command.
2. **Runs every `script:` rule** with `systemix rules check`: each rule carries its own pattern.
3. **Answers every `review:` rule** by looking at the work: hierarchy, tone, clarity.
4. **Scores the goal's evals**, when the work belongs to a goal.

## The result

| Result | Means |
|---|---|
| **fail** | A `block` rule broke. Fix it before review or publishing |
| **pass with flags** | Only `flag` rules broke. Fix them, or say why each is fine |
| **pass** | Ready for review |

Each finding reads `ID · file:line · what · fix`, blocks first, and the reply ends with the one
thing to fix first.

**If a rule looks wrong, don't work round it.** Change it in `design/rules.md` with a sample of bad
work, prove it with `systemix rules eval`, and every command picks it up on its next run.
[Fix the rule, not the instance →](/docs/concepts/rules#fix-the-rule-not-the-instance)

## Options

- `--score`: your AI-readiness score out of 100 and per area, every caveat that costs points and
  one recommendation for each. Deterministic: the same code always gives the same numbers.
- `--drift`: raw colours, sizes and spacing in your code that should reference `design/tokens.css`,
  by severity.
- `--deep`: the long, scored readiness audit, with its published rubric.
