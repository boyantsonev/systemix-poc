Builds the design system your agents will read, as files you own. **Nothing is written until you
say yes**: setup shows each file, or its diff, first.

```
/systemix:setup [--from code|interview|claude-design <link>] [--loops]
```

## Where it starts from

Setup picks the door from what is in your repo, and says which and why:

| Your repo has | Door | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| UI code, no `design/` | **From the code** | It infers the tokens and patterns you already ship, finds drift and duplicate components, and proposes a `design/` starter from them |
| No UI yet | **By interview** | A short conversation about your product, your people and the associations behind your palette, then drafts in your own voice |
| A Claude Design link or export | **From Claude Design** | Its tokens and component styles into `design/` |
| `design/` already | Straight to the rules | — |

## The rules

1. `systemix rules init` writes a starter [`design/rules.md`](/docs/concepts/rules): twelve
   generic rules for tokens, components, accessibility and voice, and one sample of bad work per
   scripted rule in `design/evals/rules/known-bad/`.
2. Setup proposes your product's own rows from `DESIGN.md`, `guardrails.mdx`, your brand's voice
   and any legal copy rules you give it, and removes starter rows that don't apply.
3. For every new scripted rule it adds a sample of bad work, and runs `systemix rules lint` and
   `systemix rules eval` until both pass. A rule whose sample is missed is not ready: the pattern
   gets fixed, not the sample.

## The loops (optional)

With `--loops`, setup also sets up the [product and build loops](/docs/concepts/loops): it asks who
holds each role, where prototypes live, how the project ships, and what the agent must never
touch, then writes `design/loops/` and checks it.

## Then

`/systemix:check <a folder of UI>` shows where your code stands against the new rules.
