---
title: For your team
description: What design, engineering, content, product and AI agents each do with Systemix.
---

A design system has always connected these roles. With Systemix they read the same files, and
every command reads the same rules, so a designer, an engineer and an agent briefed on Monday
are briefed from one source.

## Design

- Run `/systemix:setup` once to turn what ships today into `design/`, then shape `DESIGN.md` and
  `rules.md` so they say what you mean.
- Hold the `design-lead` role in the loops: you shape the pictures and approve what goes into the
  system.
- When a check flags something you'd allow, change the rule, not the screen:
  [fix the rule, not the instance](/docs/concepts/rules#fix-the-rule-not-the-instance).

## Engineering

- Run `/systemix:check` on your branch before review; `--drift` lists raw values that should be
  tokens.
- Put `systemix rules check` in CI so a block rule fails the build, the same way a test does.
- Hold the `engineering-lead` role: you approve the plan, the pull request and the release.

## Content and marketing

- Voice, banned words and legal lines are rows in `rules.md` with `copy` tests, so a post, an
  email or a landing section is checked the same way a screen is:
  `/systemix:check --surface marketing <file>`.
- Brief first: `/systemix:brief a launch email for …` gives the lines you may and may not use.

## Product and business

- A goal is one sentence: `/systemix:loop open <sentence>`. You approve its evals, decide "build it"
  at validate and "ship it" at the end, each from a brief that names the facts.
- The decision log in every goal folder answers "why is it like this" without a meeting.

## AI agents

- Agents read `design/` and `rules.md` directly; the plugin's commands are how Claude Code uses
  them, and the files are plain enough for any other agent.
- Agents may brief and check on their own. They never rule: every gate waits for a person, and
  `systemix loops check` refuses a ruling made by an agent.
